02 What I build

Four things, done to the end.

01 — AI systems & evaluation

Decide from evidence, then check the result.

  • A router that sends each coding job to the model that benchmarks best for it, with quota-aware fallback when a subscription runs out.
  • Confidence that is itself measured: does "sure" turn out right? Calibrated against real outcomes.
  • A deterministic layer of small gauges that answer from facts they can point to, and escalate to a model only when they cannot.
  • Benchmark and judge harnesses with their pitfalls written down, and claims retracted when a re-run did not reproduce them.

02 — Systems engineering

Rust that holds up on someone else's machine.

  • An async engine (axum, SQLite) that runs five different command-line AI tools as live sessions, on Windows and Linux.
  • Process and terminal orchestration, streaming over server-sent events, deadlock-safe database access.
  • A TypeScript engine ported to Rust under conformance fixtures and byte-parity tests, so behaviour did not drift.
  • About 360,000 lines of Rust and more than 5,800 engine tests.

03 — Front end & product design

Interfaces that stay fast and make sense.

  • React and TypeScript, about 100,000 lines and 3,400 tests; runtime-switchable themes on one set of design tokens.
  • A conflict-safe code editor, a Linear-style issue tracker, live-markdown notes, a code-analysis cockpit.
  • A shared stream hub so a dozen live panes stay under the browser's per-host connection cap.
  • Onboarding that checks which AI tools you have and leads you to the missing ones.

04 — Shipping & operations

From commit to a customer's desktop.

  • Tauri desktop packaging; the Windows build cross-compiled from Linux; signed installers and an auto-updater.
  • Licensing with a trial, provider-validated keys and offline signed serials, designed so a paying user is never locked out.
  • Payments integration, landing site, technical paper, newsletter, privacy and terms, release notes.
  • Clean-machine testing that found, and fixed, the Windows-only failures before customers did.

05 Method

How I work.

  1. 01

    Measure before claiming.

    A claim without a measurement is not a claim. I keep the numbers that argue against me: a cost advantage I had published was removed when a re-run no longer reproduced it.

  2. 02

    Deterministic first.

    Rules decide what rules can decide, and say what they can point to. A model is for the questions about meaning, with a measured miss rate.

  3. 03

    Never lock out a legitimate user.

    Protection is designed around the customer who pays and reinstalls on a new laptop. Offline grace, honest fallbacks, no secrets in the binary.

  4. 04

    Small, tested steps, written rules.

    One change at a time, a test before the claim, builds capped by memory, nothing pushed or released without approval. AI agents work inside rules I write down, and the rules survive the session.

  5. 05

    Ship, then listen.

    Every release is checked as a stranger would download it, then tested on a clean machine. What breaks there is the roadmap.
